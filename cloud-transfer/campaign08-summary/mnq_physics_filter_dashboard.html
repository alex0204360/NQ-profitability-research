Math and physics hypothesis · November 3, 2026

Did persistence and drift–diffusion improve the $8 gate?

No. A two-bar signal debounce and a past-only normal drift–diffusion estimate were fixed before scoring. The model estimated the probability that ten minutes of price movement would clear $6 stressed MNQ costs. That estimate was poorly calibrated, and the filtered trading results were worse than the unchanged gate on the longer window.

The hypothesis

Persistence asks whether the frozen $8 direction survives two consecutive completed bars. Diffusion estimates the last 31 one-minute price changes, then projects a ten-minute normal distribution while adding uncertainty in the estimated drift. It keeps a gate signal only when the modeled chance of exceeding three index points in that direction is above 60%. This is a falsifiable approximation, not a physical law governing markets.

What failed

High modeled probability did not imply a high observed win rate on the longer window. In its 80–100% probability bin, the mean forecast was 85.9%, while only 35.3% of matured gate opportunities cleared stressed costs. The 60% filter therefore selected the wrong opportunities; persistence also amplified losses. Adjusting that cutoff or reversing the signal now would optimize an already opened sample.
