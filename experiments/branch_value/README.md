# Does a reliable next-close warning give the position a better exit?

At the first favorable, discretionary decision on an original trade, next-close reversals were rare. On the external next-open panel only 5 of 272 such decisions were immediately followed by a reversal. On the discovery panel the count was 4 of 218. A probability estimator that distinguishes imminent changes across all eligible minutes has little economic leverage at this particular decision.

On the 60-session next-open panel, the grid-weighted branch model made 219 early exits and reduced net by $176.50 versus retaining the original exit. It finished at −$3,997.50 at $6 friction and −$5,620.50 at $9. With one extra minute it reduced net by $306. On the 40-session panel it reduced net by $188.50 and $1,191.50 at the respective delays. The zero-point forecast had lower MSE on both panels and both schedules. The frozen design did not qualify for a full re-entry test. Exact labels entered the live memory only after both the reversal and original exit matured. The retrospective control is explicitly separated in the ledger.
