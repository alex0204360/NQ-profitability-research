# Can the frozen reservoir withstand a reversal from its own remaining stock?

Freeze before any scores from this experiment. Continue on the same 40 and 60 exposed sessions. Preserve the November 19 explanatory case and both original prospective studies. The prior experiments tested price-path repairs and learned holding-time estimates. This experiment intervenes directly on the parent computation to measure how fragile its sign is.

Let f be the normalized parent forecast, S its unspent directionless stock, s its currently known encoder scale and a=7/8. For any hypothetical innovation-free path (zero new charge), the parent satisfies f'=a(f+S m), S'=a S(1−|m|) with motion clipped to [−1,1]. After n steps the reachable terminal forecast is in a^n[f−S,f+S], in normalized units. The bound follows because a unit of original stock can release at most once. Both endpoints are attainable by immediately releasing all stock in one direction and then waiting. A neutral path gives a^n f, so a sign reversal cannot come from mere exponential decay. Use a fixed one-step adverse motion of half one range as a less severe stress. The signed margin for either side is a*s*(side*f−S/2). Freeze s in this hypothetical one-step probe. A new deposit cannot affect forecast on that same step. The margin describes a counterfactual in score points; it is not a calibrated return. Large unspent stock can overturn a seemingly decisive forecast.
