# Research decisions

The November 19 runtime resumed with no local project files. The original November 12 run numbers survive in the conversation. We reconstructed the four numerical circuits from their specified matrices and fetched the exact pinned NQ file. The first three runs are replications. v03 was designed before interruption. The entire session uses exploratory prices only through 2026-04-24 UTC. The 163,620 later rows are excluded from prediction and selection. Their prior historical exposure is unknown.

1. v00. Predict continuation of the smaller of two consecutive four-bar signed displacements. Original result was -$15.973/NQ trade. Downward persistence had a subsequent positive mean move. Reject continuation.
2. v01. Add rectified close-to-extreme rejection and require coincidence with the persistence neuron. Reverse the head. Original result was -$15.780/NQ trade. Exhaustion cells fired on roughly 8% of rows each, reducing trade count without covering costs. Reject rejection readout.
3. v02. Replace all layers with temporal surprise: subtract extrapolated fourteen-bar velocity from the latest two-bar move, apply dead zone and saturation, predict partial reversal. Original -$12.666/NQ trade. Average gross +0.092 points was far below 0.725 points cost. Extreme input amplitude made saturation necessary. Reject deployment.
4. v03. Subtract absolute background velocity at each novelty neuron. Test whether removing novelty embedded in sustained movement concentrates reversible shocks. Keep original horizon, output gain and cost assumptions.

5. Executed v03. Background inhibition cut the continuous-window NQ count to 47,534. Its gross gain was 0.109 points per trade against 0.725 points of costs. Causal mean net was -$11.98. Changing the participation of the same impulse channels had little economic effect. Reject v03.

6. v04 before execution. Replace the shock hypothesis with a price-level memory. In a chart that visits A, departs to B, revisits C near A, and then starts moving away again at T, estimate room to the previous excursion B. The last layer predicts the smaller of remaining room and the departure already visible, reduced by the error between the two remembered base levels. Reverse all signed displacements for the mirror-image short. Use eight minutes because the first excursion lies about eight bars behind the decision. The horizon is a design guess. Complete numerical matrix assignments are in weights/v04.json. No calibration or sweep.

7. v04 activated on just 1.10% of rows long and 1.05% short. Highest active quartiles had subsequent signed moves +0.673 long and +0.178 short. The short channel expected the opposite sign. Causal net -$12.83 per trade, 1,739 trades. Reject.

8. v05 before execution. Return to v03, where the up-overload neuron fired on 1.7% of bars. Its four active quartiles had future signed moves +0.61, +0.25, +0.60, +1.13 points, all opposite the reversal head. Down-overload had no mirrored response. Silence the moderate and downward pathways. Reroute positive overload directly to a positive linear readout. Subtract upper-distance with weight -1 as local evidence that the last bar surrendered part of the displacement. Saturate the final output at one range unit through a parallel row with bias -3 rather than -2. This predeclares one mechanistic long-only edit and preserves H4. An always-long background move, day concentration and roll artifacts can mimic success. Inspect those directly.

9. v05 reached +0.670 gross NQ points and -$1.11 net across 2,796 causal trades. Active overload quartiles changed from -0.110 to +1.378 future points, but one April 2025 winner alone contributed $8,025.50 versus -$3,102 overall. Results by year changed sign. The cut at a two-minute sum cannot distinguish a two-bar advance from one isolated jump followed by inactivity or surrender. In v06 require that both one-minute displacements support the joint impulse. Add parallel opponent-rectified inputs for the two latest advances, then implement y=min(v05_output, 2*relu(previous_return), 2*relu(last_return)) with copy-minus-ReLU cells. Two is the natural conversion from a one-minute to a two-minute displacement. Same gain, horizon and costs. Test one structural edit, without choosing thresholds from quartile results.

10. v06 produced +$0.76 causal NQ/trade, first half +$2.83 and second -$0.93. Its 0.763 gross points did not exceed the descriptive same-date/same-hour long reference of 1.157. Remove the best date and net mean becomes -$2.55. A possible confound is general upside during the hours of activation. This comparison uses hindsight for diagnosis, never as a trading input. Hold back deployment.

11. Final bounded experiment v07. Move the two-step impulse from the latest pair of minutes to the preceding pair. Give the network a two-bar delay line. Use the oldest twelve minutes at weight 1/6 to express a background two-minute movement. During the two-bar wait, displacement in either direction consumes the stored signal: retreat means failure to hold the new level, advance spends the hypothesized remaining distance. Subtract absolute delayed displacement and present upper distance from the bounded, two-step-confirmed old impulse, then rectify. Predict the next four minutes after this decision. No grid over delays, gains or horizons. End this eight-version exploratory series after this test and its attribution checks.

12. v07 causal net -$38.52/NQ trade, 292 trades, mean gross -1.201 points. Both halves and all calendar years negative. Highest held-impulse quartile had +0.039 future points. Reject the delayed hold interpretation. This completes eight numerical programs. Preserve v06 for mechanistic study, select flat for execution, and leave the later period unused. No independent validation was attempted.
