A transient adverse impulse should lose statistical evidence on recovery. A series of adverse bodies may retain enough evidence to retire the trade. Bouchaud et al. motivate a distinction between transient and persistent price response using signed trades and quotes in stocks. Minute bodies are only a proxy. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0307332)

At the entry open initialize three evidence capitals to one. For each completed bar, clip its body divided by the prior 32-bar exponential range to [-1,1] and multiply by minus position sign. Before observing that body, the preceding bar's volume relative to its lagging background multiplies the odds of each stake. Three wealth cells multiply their prior wealth by one plus the adjusted stake times the adverse body. Fixed weights [1/4,1/2,1/4] pool the capital. The control holds prior volume odds at one. One high-volume down bar alone cannot stop a trade. Positive Hold value favors either current position until the original sign dies, the pooled evidence reaches eight, or the original gap or clock exit. Hold value is the smaller of signed raw forecast and prior range times log2(8/evidence), in points. This is an experimental stopping margin, with no calibrated return interpretation. Warm state resets at gaps and during flat intervals.

Under zero conditional mean of the signed clipped body, the wealth mixture is a nonnegative martingale starting at one and the chance of ever crossing eight is at most 1/8 per trade under that null.
