# A volume network for forced inventory transfer

Freeze this graph before replay. Replace the directional encoder, opportunity detector, hold readout and exit. Preserve the original as a control. Estimate an inventory cohort for each completed candle using its volume and close location. The close-location row [2,-1,-1] on close, high and low proxies asymmetric participation; OHLCV cannot identify aggressors or actual stops. Use only the excess buyer or seller mass, measured in units of a prior mean volume. Give a cohort a uniform cost distribution across its high/low. Hypothesize that a participant becomes vulnerable after one range of adverse travel. Each slice of inventory can release a forced order only once, on a new adverse closing-price excursion. An inventory of trapped shorts releases buy flow, trapped longs sell flow. Retire old cohorts after 32 bars and decay remaining mass with half-life eight baseline volumes. Compute current forced flow before depositing the current cohort. The release is a proxy for continuation, not an observed liquidation.

The second layer subtracts favorable travel from unspent point voltage, leaks the remainder in volume time, and adds signed square-root impact of the force imbalance times the prior range. Admit entries only when absolute modeled remaining travel exceeds the three points of round-trip friction. Exit at the next open when held-side score falls to half a tick or lower. Nonzero-range zero-volume trade bars reset the new graph and require a fresh 32-bar warmup. This is a quality rule for the new strategy, not a retrospective repair of the original. All controls of the new graph get the identical data-quality mask.

Primary candidate uses observed volume. Ablations use equal volume for every usable candle and, separately, no sided cohort allocation (half the normalized volume to each side).
