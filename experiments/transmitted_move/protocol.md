# Attribute a two-venue gap closure before forecasting common price

The minute-level NQ–MNQ discrepancy ranked movement into the next opening print, but it failed at nine seconds and in the next executable minute. A different hypothesis concerns how price *converges*. A gap can disappear because a lagging contract moves to the leader or because an initial price change is withdrawn. Test whether these observed paths imply different subsequent MNQ returns. Freeze before classifying second-level episodes or reading their future returns.

Use only the two pin-verified second OHLCV files in the source audit, 27 prespecified previously exposed regular-session dates, one MNQ, no quote or queue assumptions. Work on consecutive seconds with a completed bar for each instrument. Reset episode memory across any missing common second, new session or unknown basis. At each second use the median of the preceding 31 completed minute spreads from the original synchronized minute archive. Accept a new episode only when the preceding completed minute exactly reconciled on both instruments, as could be checked after its closure. Keep basis fixed during a live episode. Mark the last synchronized pair with absolute basis-adjusted gap ≤0.25 as the anchor. When the gap magnitude reaches at least 1.0 point, identify the leader as the contract with the larger absolute displacement from that anchor. Reject ties and events where the leader moved against the divergence or had zero displacement. Require the anchor no older than 30 seconds. Define D as the sign of that leader's move, orient the spread from lagger to leader so the initial gap has positive magnitude, and store both prices.

Use a maximum lifetime of 30 seconds from opening. Replace both stored prices only at a strictly larger oriented gap. At the first subsequent second when that gap is ≤0.25, let c=D*(current lagger−lagger at peak), w=−D*(current leader−leader at peak), k=c+w. Then k is exactly the gap closed from the peak in points. Form q=clip((c−w)/k,−1,1). Issue a continuation in direction D only if q≥0.5 (lagger supplies at least three quarters of closure); issue a fade in direction −D if q≤−0.5. Otherwise abstain. Reset after closure, counting it as a fresh anchor if within 0.25. This signed attribution is proportional to the midpoint change from the peak; it does not identify trade initiators. Assess continuation and fade separately as well as their prespecified combined rule.

After each complete signal second, test entries after 0, 1, 9 and 60 additional seconds, with a fixed 60-second open-to-open hold, an entry proxy at the first MNQ observed open no more than two seconds from scheduled entry, and scheduled exit 60 seconds after the actual entry. Compare actual opening and closing times against requested times; release a missing entry and flatten an open trade at the first following observed MNQ open. Report late exits (>2 seconds) or unresolved exits and bar-data limits; unresolved exposure blocks promotion. Only issue when even the 60-second-delayed entry plus two seconds tolerance and holding interval can fit by 15:55. Use independent one-position ledgers; ignore signals while pending or occupied. Controls on identical candidate timings: always follow the original leader, always long, sign of the last MNQ second body (flat on equality), and sign of total two-contract midpoint displacement since the anchor. Cash costs zero. Include all signals as descriptive overlapping return labels separately from the executable policies. Publish event state and accepted trades, net/gross, drawdown, concentration and paired session-block uncertainty. A precondition for prospective examination is ≥50 filled trades in 15 sessions, positive stressed net without the top 3 trades, $6 upper observed drawdown below $1,500, and positive lower five-session paired interval vs cash and simple directions at both 1 and 9 seconds. No strategy or risk claim follows solely from one-second OHLCV observations.
